# Milestone 1 — Community and candidate distinctions

**Community:** r/chess (public subreddit). Posts are comments from the
threads about the Carlsen–Niemann cheating controversy.

**What I read:** ~40 comments across several of those threads before
deciding anything.

**Candidate distinctions I saw in the text:**

1. Some comments back their point with something checkable: a number of
   games, a date, a quote from a named person, a link, or actual chess
   moves. Most comments assert an opinion with nothing to check.
2. Some comments are not sincere at all: jokes, sarcasm, one-liners,
   parody. The community treats them differently from real arguments.
3. Some comments contain numbers that are *not* checkable: personal
   estimates ("<10%"); anecdotes, which look like evidence but aren't.
4. Comments are about the people (Magnus, Hans) as often as about the
   chess itself; tone is heated either way, so tone is not a usable
   distinction.
